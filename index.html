Build your understanding

The Psychology Library

Begin with foundational concepts and explore how psychology studies thoughts, feelings, actions, relationships, and human development.

Introduction to Psychology

Psychology is the scientific study of behaviour and mental processes. Researchers use different methods to investigate learning, emotion, attention, perception, memory, motivation, and social interaction.

Major perspectives include biological, cognitive, behavioural, developmental, social, cultural, and psychodynamic approaches. Each can help explain some aspects of human experience, but none explains everything.

Emotions and Emotional Regulation

Emotions involve interacting physiological, cognitive, behavioural, and subjective processes. Emotional regulation refers to ways people influence how emotions develop, how they are experienced, and how they are expressed.

Strategies can include identifying an emotion, considering context, changing an unhelpful situation where possible, and seeking support. Suppressing emotion is not always helpful or harmful; outcomes depend on the situation.

Learning, Memory, and Attention

Learning involves changes associated with experience. Memory includes processes such as encoding, storage, and retrieval. Attention helps prioritise information, but it has limits and is influenced by context.

Retrieval practice, spaced learning, sleep, and appropriately challenging practice can support learning. No single study technique works equally well for every task.

Development Across the Lifespan

Developmental psychology investigates changes in physical, cognitive, emotional, and social functioning throughout life. Development is shaped by interacting biological, environmental, relational, and cultural influences.

Developmental theories offer frameworks for understanding patterns, but individual lives do not always follow a single fixed timeline.

Social and Cultural Psychology

People are influenced by relationships, group norms, social roles, institutions, culture, and historical circumstances. Social psychology examines how these influences interact with individual processes.

Cultural context matters when interpreting behaviour. Avoid assuming that a finding from one population applies identically to every society.

Psychological Research and Evidence

Psychological research uses methods including experiments, surveys, interviews, observation, and longitudinal studies. Different methods answer different questions and have different limitations.

Correlation alone does not establish causation. Sample size, measurement quality, bias, replication, and uncertainty matter when interpreting findings.

Personality and Individual Differences

Personality research examines patterns in how people tend to think, feel, and behave. Traits are tendencies, not complete descriptions of a person or predictions of every action.

Personality questionnaires should not automatically be treated as diagnoses or unquestionable statements about identity.

Neuroscience and the Brain

Neuroscience investigates the nervous system and its relationships with behaviour and cognition. Brain functions emerge through interacting networks rather than one isolated region responsible for every complex experience.

Brain imaging and biological explanations can be useful, but they require careful interpretation and do not automatically explain an individual's experience.

Care and responsibility

Learning with care

Hey Lil Bird is an educational project. Its content is not a diagnosis, personalised medical advice, or a substitute for professional mental-health care.

Psychological research can be uncertain, culturally limited, or misinterpreted. Consider the quality of evidence and the context in which findings were obtained.

You do not have to share private experiences to belong here. Avoid publishing identifying information, passwords, private medical details, or someone else's personal story without permission.

If you are experiencing persistent distress, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or a trusted person who can help you in person.

A future public community should include moderation, reporting, privacy controls, and clear rules against harassment, exploitation, and harmful misinformation.
