Start here · Psychology

What is psychology?

Psychology is the scientific study of behavior and mental processes.

Let's make it human

Why do people remember some things and forget others? Why do we become attached to people? Why can the same event affect two people differently? Psychology asks questions like these and uses systematic methods to investigate them.

Go a little deeper

Psychology includes many fields and perspectives. Researchers study cognition, emotion, development, personality, social behavior, health, learning and the relationship between biological, psychological and social influences.

Important: Psychology is much larger than therapy. Psychologists also conduct research, assessment, education, consultation and work across health, education, organizations, technology and other settings.

Want the science?

Psychological claims can be evaluated through experiments, observational studies, surveys, interviews, longitudinal research, case studies and other methods. Different questions require different designs.

Brain & behavior

What is neuroscience?

Neuroscience studies the nervous system, including the brain, spinal cord, neurons and the biological processes that support behavior and mental functions.

What does it explore?

Neuroscience can examine memory, learning, movement, perception, emotion, attention, sleep and communication between nerve cells. It connects biology with questions about behavior and experience.

A useful boundary

The brain matters enormously, but human behavior is not explained by one brain region, one neurotransmitter or one “chemical.” Psychological, biological, social and environmental factors can interact.

Lil Bird rule: We won't turn neuroscience into motivational slogans. If evidence is uncertain or mixed, the page should say so.

Mental health

Understanding without labeling yourself.

Mental health includes wellbeing and the ability to cope, learn, work, connect and participate in life. Mental-health difficulties can take many forms and vary across people and circumstances.

Everyday experiences

Stress · worry · loneliness · grief · burnout · self-esteem · sleep · emotional regulation · life transitions

Education

Anxiety · depression · trauma-related conditions · OCD · eating disorders · bipolar disorders · psychotic disorders · and more

Support

Self-reflection, trusted people, qualified professionals, community support and crisis services when appropriate.

Education is not diagnosis. A list of symptoms cannot tell you whether you have a mental disorder. Lil Bird can explain concepts and help you find appropriate support.

How we know

Psychology isn't just a collection of facts.

It is a science that uses different methods to investigate different questions.

Experiments

Useful for testing causal questions under controlled conditions.

Correlational research

Measures relationships between variables but does not by itself establish causation.

Longitudinal research

Follows people or groups over time to examine change.

Qualitative research

Explores meaning, experience and context through methods such as interviews and observation.

Systematic reviews

Bring together evidence using explicit methods to answer focused questions.

Replication

Repeating research helps test how robust findings are.

Everyday psychology

The comparison window

Imagine checking your phone after a difficult day and seeing someone else's carefully edited highlight reel. Your mind begins comparing. The story is not “social media causes depression.” The useful question is: what psychological processes might be happening here?

Reflection

The pause before the answer

Sometimes the most useful next step is not another piece of advice. It is a small pause that gives you enough room to notice what you actually need.

Safety by design

Kindness needs boundaries.

Lil Bird will not: diagnose, prescribe medication, give instructions for self-harm or violence, encourage secrecy, impersonate a human professional, or promise absolute confidentiality.

Lil Bird will: be honest about uncertainty, prioritize safety, encourage appropriate human support, minimize data collection, and route high-risk situations through defined safety procedures.
